The board in Milford does not look like the one they printed in the preview.

Jake Monn, a 1028-rated name out of Simpsonville, S.C., is at 15 under after two rounds of the PDGA Professional World Championships. Aaron Gossage is a stroke back. Gannon Buhr, last year’s champion, is tied for fifth. And a Baton Rouge name — Silas Schultz — is tied for third.

That is Worlds week. Ten days of it, if you count the walk from here to Idlewild and then into the playoffs. The livestream is in Michigan. The heat is here. Highland Road Park is still the first permanent course this city ever built, still the one people drive past on the way to something prettier, still the one built to be filmed.

This paper starts there.

Worlds is a five-day major on two Kensington courses in Milford, Mich. It is not a coronation. After two rounds it is a short list of names the preview did not bother to bold.

Monn shot 57–59 and sits at −15. Gossage is −14. Schultz, listed out of Baton Rouge, is −12 with Anthony Barela. Buhr is one of five at −11. The Saturday cut still has to happen. Sunday’s finals are on Toboggan. The week is not over.

In FPO, Eveliina Salonen posted a 59 in the second round and leads at −14. Missy Gannon is three back. Ohn Scoggins, last year’s champion, is tied for 12th at −2 — twelve strokes off the lead. Iida Lehtomäki, a 959-rated name out of Finland, is alone in third at −9. The unexpected names are the story. The rest is a 10-day sprint that has not finished the first week.

In the Bag

The Buzzz

CryZtal Nate Doss Reissue Buzzz, 2026 Worlds. DGPT shop sold out. Buy: Infinite. Source: Infinite, best midranges 2026.

One mold this month. The Discraft Buzzz.

Worlds bag features were not up at deadline. No counts. No invented cards. What is up is the shelf. Worlds week is selling this midrange two ways: a CryZtal Nate Doss reissue, etched-top, 5 / 5 / −1 / 1, and a first official Innova × Discraft pack that puts a Roc and a Buzzz in the same sealed box. The manufacturers can talk about purse support. The copy is simpler. This is the mid people actually throw.

Infinite Discs’ mid-year numbers put the Buzzz more than double the second-place Hex in 2025 sales, still on top in the first half of 2026, and the highest-rated mid in their reviews — 587 of them. Twenty-two years in the bin. Beadless rim. A touch of turn, a bit of fade. You can shape it. You already own one that is too beat, and you will buy another because the stamp is Worlds.

Shop: Infinite, in stock ($10.99–$27.99). The DGPT CryZtal Doss is sold out. The Innova × Discraft Worlds pack is $49.99.

That is the mold. Not a lifestyle object. A workhorse that happens to be on the table this week.

On the Course

Highland still has the hills

Hole 10 gold, Highland Road Park, looking back to the tee. Dual pins. DHollier73, DGCR, Nov. 2019. UDisc · BREC.

Highland is already Jomez caliber. People do not give it its due.

It does not need a new capital project. It needs love — care, routing, respect. Then it belongs on the card.

Baton Rouge’s first permanent course went in here in 1987. Not the first in Louisiana. The first in this city. BREC rebuilt it once, the real rebuild, May 2019 to February 2020: drainage, bridges, pads, signs, dual purple and gold. The March 2020 opening was canceled for COVID. That is the history. It is not a case against the place.

There is no 2024–26 disc-golf capital project on the books. Current BREC work at the park is tennis, through fall 2026. What is coming is a management handoff. On Talk 107.3 last September, Janet Simmons said BRDGA will manage Highland. Management. Not a rebuild.

Club talk has run ahead of the parish. BRDGA’s January bag-tag copy mentioned “new hole ideas for the renovation.” UDisc still carries an inactive 7,079-foot, par-62 layout from that weekend. That is club talk. It is not a BREC bid.

The public already voted with the app. UDisc has Highland at 4.4 from about 2,110 reviews, No. 6 in the state. Parc des Familles is No. 1 in Louisiana in 2026, 4.7, and it already has the Jomez card — The Big Easy. UDisc and PDGA both mark it flat. Cypress corridors. Pretty. Featureless. Highland has the elevation.

Walk hole 10 gold. BREC’s map calls it 726 feet, looking back to the tee. The Battle of the Bayou 2024 caddie had it 747, creek left, road long of the basket. One hole. One line. That is the picture. A course that already has the bones, waiting on the care.
